Resistance $R$ is the opposition of [[Current]]. For uniform resistivity, it can be defined using [[Resistivity]], the length of a wire, and the cross-sectional area.
$$
R=\frac{\rho L}{A}
$$
This formula can be found from $\Delta V =\displaystyle -\int_{i}^{f} \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r}$.
$$
\vec{E}  = \rho \vec{j}, \hspace{ 0.2in } \vec{E} = E\hat{i}_{x}, \hspace{ 0.2in } \vec{j} = j \hat{i}_{x}, \hspace{ 0.2in }\Rightarrow E=\rho j
$$
$$
\begin{align}
\Delta V & = - \int_{1}^{2} \vec{E} \cdot \, d\vec{r} = - \int_{0}^{L} E \, dx = - \int_{0}^{L} \rho j \, dx =- \int_{0}^{L} \rho \frac{i}{A} \, dx \\
 & = - \rho  \frac{i}{A} L = -EL , V(L)-V(0)= -V\\
\end{align}
$$
Following this, we find [[Ohm's Law]].
$$
\begin{align}
\implies V & = i \rho  \frac{L}{A}, R= \rho  \frac{L}{A} \\
\implies \Aboxed{ V & = iR }
\end{align}
$$

Resistance is measured in $\Omega$ (Ohms).

In [[Circuits]], resistors are used to oppose the current.
