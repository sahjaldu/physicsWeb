The definition of work $W$ is
$$
W = \int_{C} \vec{F}(\vec{s}) \cdot  d\vec{s}
$$
Where $d\vec{s}$ is a small change in the displacement vector along path $C$. It can be interpreted as the accumulated effect of a force along a path. Here we use the [[Dot Product]].

Another interpretation is that work is a measure of how much energy is transferred in or out of a system.

The $\mathrm{SI}$ unit for work is the newton-meter ($\mathrm{Nm}$) or the Joule $\mathrm{J}$. When dealing with subatomic particles we use the electron-volt $\mathrm{\ eV}$ where $1\mathrm{\ eV}=1.60\times 1^{-19}\mathrm{\ J}$.

For work to happen, there must be a $\vec{F}$ in the $\vec{s}$ direction or $\vec{s}$ in the $\vec{F}$ direction. When the force is opposite the direction, there is negative work.

For [[Rotational Motion]], for a rotation from $\theta_{i}$ to $\theta_{f}$, work is:
$$
\begin{align}
dW&=(F\sin\theta)ds \\
&=(F\sin\theta)(r\ d\theta) \\
&=(rF\sin\theta)d\theta \\
\Aboxed{W&=\int_{\theta_{i}}^{\theta_{f}}\vec{\tau} \cdot \vec{\theta}}
\end{align}
$$
